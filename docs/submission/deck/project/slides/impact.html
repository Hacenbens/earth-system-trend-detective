What it means

  • Eastern Sahel (Niger–Chad): wetter, more regular rainy seasons, a hopeful signal for rain-fed farming
  • Western Sahel: no recovery so far
  • Single years still swing hard: 2024 was the wettest season in 29 years (+20 %), 2026 was dry (−10 %)

How far to trust it

  • Every claim is in a public evidence ledger, with its data, test and limits
  • Checked against NASA Giovanni and an independent rainfall record
  • Limits: only 28 years, a reanalysis for the atmosphere, and heavy-rain records changed with the 2014 satellite upgrade, so no claim about extremes
  • AI assistance is logged, as the challenge rules require

The eastern Sahel’s rainy season has become wetter and more regular, a hopeful signal for farmers in Niger and Chad. But the west is not recovering, and single years still swing hard: 2024 was the wettest season in our record and 2026 was dry. We are explicit about our limits: twenty-eight years is short, the atmospheric data come from a reanalysis, and the heaviest-rain records changed with the 2014 satellite upgrade, so we make no claim about extremes. Everything is open: the code, the data checks, and an evidence ledger behind every number.

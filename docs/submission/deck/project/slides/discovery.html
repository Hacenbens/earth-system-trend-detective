Sahel-wide: a tendency only (+3.3 %/decade, p = 0.15) · East partly driven by the wet 2024 season (without it: +7.0 %, p = 0.067) · NASA GPM IMERG V07, CHC CHIRPS v3 · AI-assisted analysis code

Averaged over the whole Sahel, rainfall shows only a weak upward tendency, which alone would be a dead end. The regional view is what matters. The east, Niger and Chad, is getting wetter by about 8 percent per decade, in NASA’s satellite record and in an independent gauge-based dataset. Even more robust, the longest dry spell inside the rainy season is shortening by about 10 percent per decade, whichever year we remove and wherever we draw the border. A spatial PCA with no borders at all finds the same east–west see-saw tilting east. Caveat to say out loud: the eastern trend is partly driven by the exceptional 2024 season.

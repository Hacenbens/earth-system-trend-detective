# Top 10 recent articles on weather and climate in the African Sahel (2023–2026)

## Purpose and selection approach

This curated reading list supports the research framing **“cloud properties and their relationship with rainfall variability and extreme precipitation over the African Sahel.”** It prioritizes recent peer-reviewed articles on convection and mesoscale convective systems (MCSs), rainfall extremes, land–atmosphere feedbacks, monsoon circulation, aerosols, sea-surface temperatures (SSTs), and climate-data choices.

All ten entries below are **peer-reviewed, published articles** (version of record), checked against the publisher or journal page. No in-press-only or preprint-only item is included. The 2026 entry is already published, not a preprint.

## Recommended articles

### 1. Tropical waves and extreme rainfall in the central Sahel

**Peyrillé, Philippe; Roehrig, Romain; and Sanogo, Sidiki (2023).** “Tropical Waves Are Key Drivers of Extreme Precipitation Events in the Central Sahel.” *Geophysical Research Letters*, 50, e2023GL103715. [https://doi.org/10.1029/2023GL103715](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023GL103715)

- **Research question:** Which large-scale tropical waves create the atmospheric environment for extreme precipitation in the central Sahel?
- **Methods/data:** Statistical composites of extreme rainfall in Burkina Faso, combined with outgoing-longwave-radiation, atmospheric moisture, winds, and wave-filtering diagnostics.
- **Key relevance:** It connects deep convection and heavy rain to equatorial Rossby waves, African easterly waves, Kelvin waves, and the Madden–Julian Oscillation. A strong paper for explaining why particular cloud/MCS events become extreme.

### 2. Attribution of the Sahel rainfall-regime intensification

**Chagnaud, Guillaume; Panthou, G.; Vischel, T.; and Lebel, T. (2023).** “Capturing and Attributing the Rainfall Regime Intensification in the West African Sahel with CMIP6 Models.” *Journal of Climate*, 36(6), 1823–1843. [https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-22-0412.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-22-0412.1)

- **Research question:** Can CMIP6 models reproduce the observed strengthening of the Sahel rainfall regime, including wet days and daily extremes, and what causes it?
- **Methods/data:** Daily regional observations are compared with coupled CMIP6, DAMIP detection-and-attribution, and AMIP atmosphere-only simulations.
- **Key relevance:** Useful for placing present-day extreme rainfall and MCS studies in a climate-change framework. The analysis finds a detectable anthropogenic contribution, with aerosol forcing important for the simulated wetting signal.

### 3. Large-scale environments favourable for MCSs

**Nkrumah, Francis; Klein, Cornelia; Quagraine, Kwesi Akumenyi; Berkoh-Oforiwaa, Rebecca; Klutse, Nana Ama Browne; Essien, Patrick; Quenum, Gandomè Mayeul Leger Davy; and Koffi, Hubert Azoda (2023).** “Classification of Large-Scale Environments That Drive the Formation of Mesoscale Convective Systems Over Southern West Africa.” *Weather and Climate Dynamics*, 4, 773–788. [https://doi.org/10.5194/wcd-4-773-2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/wcd-4-773-2023)

- **Research question:** Which recurring synoptic environments favour MCS formation in southern West Africa, and how are these environments related to the monsoon and the Sahel?
- **Methods/data:** Self-organizing maps applied to reanalysis fields and satellite-derived MCS occurrence, yielding nine synoptic states.
- **Key relevance:** MCSs are cloud systems that carry much of regional rainfall. The paper identifies the roles of moisture, low-level westerlies, vertical wind shear, and northern Sahel warming—directly useful for selecting atmospheric predictors of cloud/rainfall events.

### 4. Land-memory effects after Sahelian MCSs

**Taylor, Christopher M.; Klein, Cornelia; and Harris, Bethan L. (2024).** “Multiday Soil Moisture Persistence and Atmospheric Predictability Resulting From Sahelian Mesoscale Convective Systems.” *Geophysical Research Letters*, 51, e2024GL109709. [https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL109709](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL109709)

- **Research question:** How long do soil-moisture patterns left by MCS rainfall persist, and can they improve prediction of subsequent convection and rainfall?
- **Methods/data:** Meteosat Second Generation cloud-top temperatures are used to identify and track MCSs (2004–2020); satellite soil-moisture, land-surface-temperature, vegetation, and rainfall observations track the post-storm response.
- **Key relevance:** An especially close match to cloud–rainfall feedback research. Wet storm tracks suppress subsequent local rain for roughly 2–8 days, while the rapidly decaying land signal constrains forecast skill.

### 5. North-eastern tropical Atlantic SST and Senegal rainfall

**Thiam, Mamadou; Oruba, Ludivine; de Coetlogon, Gaelle; Wade, Malick; Diop, Bouya; and Farota, Abdou Karim (2024).** “Impact of the Sea Surface Temperature in the North-Eastern Tropical Atlantic on Precipitation Over Senegal.” *Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres*, 129, e2023JD040513. [https://doi.org/10.1029/2023JD040513](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023JD040513)

- **Research question:** How do north-eastern tropical Atlantic SST anomalies influence July–September rainfall over Senegal?
- **Methods/data:** Analysis of 1979–2018 CRU precipitation and ERA5 reanalysis, including circulation, pressure, moisture transport, the intertropical convergence zone, African easterly jet, and heat-low diagnostics.
- **Key relevance:** Provides a concrete western-Sahel mechanism linking ocean conditions to atmospheric moisture convergence, convection, and rainfall. It is valuable when adding SST predictors to a cloud–rainfall study.

### 6. Aerosol forcing of Sahel summer precipitation

**Guo, Jianing; Xie, Xiaoning; Myhre, Gunnar; Shindell, Drew; Kirkevåg, Alf; Iversen, Trond; Samset, Bjørn H.; Shi, Zhengguo; Li, Xinzhou; Sun, Hui; Liu, Xiaodong; and Liu, Yangang (2024).** “Increased Asian Sulfate Aerosol Emissions Remarkably Enhance Sahel Summer Precipitation.” *Earth’s Future*, 12(11), e2024EF004745. [https://doi.org/10.1029/2024EF004745](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024EF004745)

- **Research question:** Did the contrasting trends in Asian and European sulfate aerosols contribute to the recent recovery of Sahel summer rainfall?
- **Methods/data:** Long observational precipitation records and MERRA-2 aerosol optical depth are analysed alongside PDRMIP idealized simulations, CMIP6 historical-attribution experiments, and future scenarios.
- **Key relevance:** Extends a cloud/rainfall review beyond local weather: aerosols alter radiation and circulation, which can change monsoon strength and moisture transport. It provides a defensible atmospheric-influence pathway to discuss alongside dust/aerosol variables.

### 7. Why extreme Sahelian rain has continued to rise

**Spät, Dorian; Biasutti, Michela; and Voigt, Aiko (2025).** “Extreme Sahelian Rainfall Continues to Rise Despite Stable Storm Frequency.” *Geophysical Research Letters*, 52(16), e2025GL115942. [https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL115942](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL115942)

- **Research question:** Why have Sahelian rainfall totals and extremes continued to increase even after strong-storm frequency stopped rising?
- **Methods/data:** Long-term observational and reanalysis precipitation records are combined with satellite-based MCS and cold-cloud-cluster metrics, wind shear, and moisture diagnostics from 1982–2024.
- **Key relevance:** This is one of the most direct recent papers for the proposed topic. It distinguishes cloud/storm frequency from storm rainfall intensity and links the recent rise in extremes to greater atmospheric moisture aloft and near the surface.

### 8. Dynamics and model representation of contrasting Sahel extremes

**Sanogo, Souleymane; Maranan, Marlon; Fink, Andreas H.; Woodhams, Beth J.; and Knippertz, Peter (2025).** “Dynamics and Model Representation of Two Contrasting Extreme Precipitation Events in the Sahel.” *Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society*, first published 21 December 2025; subsequently assigned to 152(776), e70084 (2026). [https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.70084](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.70084)

- **Research question:** What dynamical settings produced two flood-causing rain events in Mali, and how well does the ICON model represent them?
- **Methods/data:** Case studies of the 8 August 2012 San and 25 August 2019 Kéniéba events, evaluating rainfall structure, African easterly waves, circulation, moisture environment, and convection-permitting versus parameterized ICON configurations.
- **Key relevance:** Demonstrates that similarly extreme rainfall can arise from different cloud/atmospheric environments. It is highly useful for case-study design and for understanding the limitations of models that parameterize convection.

### 9. Future Sahel precipitation uncertainty from extratropical SSTs

**Monerie, Paul-Arthur; Mohino, Elsa; Moine, Marie-Pierre; Biasutti, Michela; Pohl, Benjamin; and Mignot, Juliette (2025).** “Exploring Uncertainty in Dynamical Future Changes in Sahel Precipitation: The Extratropical Influence.” *Climate Dynamics*, 63, Article 353. [https://doi.org/10.1007/s00382-025-07835-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00382-025-07835-0)

- **Research question:** How do uncertain changes in North Atlantic and Mediterranean SSTs create uncertainty in future Sahel rainfall and heavy precipitation?
- **Methods/data:** CMIP6 storylines and targeted SST sensitivity experiments in three atmosphere models; precipitation changes are decomposed into dynamic and thermodynamic components.
- **Key relevance:** Shows how large-scale circulation and moisture responses can alter the meteorological setting for Sahel rainfall extremes. It gives physical context for interpreting cloud/MCS statistics under climate change.

### 10. Choosing precipitation data for Sahel climate and extremes

**Muhammad, Wada Idris; Wu, Zhiyong; Gebresellase, Selamawit Haftu; Ibrahim, Abba; and Badji, Ousmane (2026).** “Multi-Criteria Framework for Evaluating Gridded Precipitation Climate Products in the Sahel Region.” *Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies*, 64, 103240. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrh.2026.103240](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrh.2026.103240)

- **Publication status:** **Peer-reviewed published article** (April 2026); not in press and not a preprint.
- **Research question:** Which gridded precipitation products are most reliable across the Sahel for climatology, drought, and extreme-rainfall applications?
- **Methods/data:** Ten gauge-derived, satellite-derived, and reanalysis precipitation products are assessed against station observations using statistical, extreme-event, and drought modules, then ranked with compromise programming, multi-criteria decision-making, and meta-analysis.
- **Key relevance:** A practical methods paper for a cloud–rainfall project. It helps justify the rainfall reference product used to evaluate relationships with Meteosat cloud-top temperature, cloud cover, or MCS metrics; it also cautions against treating reanalysis rainfall as a ground-truth proxy.

## Suggested reading order for a cloud–rainfall study

1. **Start with MCS and extremes:** Peyrillé et al. (2023), Taylor et al. (2024), Spät et al. (2025), and Sanogo et al. (2025).
2. **Add environmental controls:** Nkrumah et al. (2023), Thiam et al. (2024), and Guo et al. (2024).
3. **Place the findings in climate context:** Chagnaud et al. (2023) and Monerie et al. (2025).
4. **Choose precipitation data carefully:** Wu et al. (2026), then document the validation approach in the methods section.

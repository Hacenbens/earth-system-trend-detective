# Research References: Sahel Rainfall, Storms, and Trend Analysis

This document organizes the 17 research papers shared through Consensus.
Each entry includes the article title, publication year, a short
plain-language description, and the original Consensus reference.

> **Note on metadata:** The Consensus pages could not be opened directly
> during preparation. Publication years and descriptions were
> cross-checked against accessible publisher, journal, repository, or
> PubMed records where possible. Where the year could not be
> independently confirmed, it is marked **Year to verify** rather than
> guessed. Descriptions are concise summaries of the topic and should
> not be treated as substitutes for reading the full paper.

## 1. Sahel and West African rainfall patterns

### 1.1 Extreme Sahelian Rainfall Continues to Rise Despite Stable Storm Frequency

-   **Authors:** Dorian Spät, Michela Biasutti, Aiko Voigt
-   **Year:** 2025
-   **Description:** Examines why extreme rainfall in the Sahel
    continues to increase even when the frequency of storms is no longer
    rising. The study links the increase to stronger rainfall intensity
    and greater atmospheric moisture.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/sp%C3%A4t-biasutti-extreme-sahelian-rainfall-continues-to-rise/w:b88b40a1-d5b0-44f4-8e10-2a2a5767d238/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL115942

### 1.2 Interannual Rainfall Variability in West Africa: Reconstruction Based on Atmospheric Circulation Patterns

-   **Authors:** Manuel Rauch, Jan Bliefernicht, Marlon Maranan,
    Andreas H. Fink, Harald Kunstmann
-   **Year:** 2025
-   **Description:** Reconstructs year-to-year rainfall variations
    across West Africa using atmospheric circulation patterns. It
    explores how large-scale atmospheric conditions can help explain and
    potentially anticipate wet and dry years.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/rauch-bliefernicht-interannual-rainfall-variability-in-west-africa/w:4f0754e3-0967-491d-8d27-4fa3ac859140/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.70088

### 1.3 An Imminent Return to Drought in the Western Sahel?

-   **Authors:** Dahirou Wane, Alessandra Giannini, Alexey Kaplan,
    Amadou T. Gaye
-   **Year:** 2025
-   **Description:** Investigates the possibility of a future shift
    toward drier conditions in the western Sahel, even while central and
    eastern parts of the Sahel may continue becoming wetter. It
    highlights how global warming, monsoon changes, and North Atlantic
    conditions can produce different regional rainfall futures.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/wane-giannini-an-imminent-return-to-drought-in-the-western-sahel/w:847e1684-e811-49e7-99e3-1331f0f8f2fe/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adu5415

### 1.4 Investigating the Influence of Vertical Moisture Integrated Flux Convergence on Rainfall Recovery in the Sahel Region of West Africa

-   **Authors:** Teslim Olatunde Adigun, Ayomide Victor Arowolo,
    Ifeoluwa Adebowale Balogun
-   **Year:** 2024
-   **Description:** Studies how the convergence and vertical movement
    of atmospheric moisture contribute to the recovery of rainfall in
    the Sahel. It is useful for understanding the atmospheric processes
    behind changes in regional precipitation.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/adigun-arowolo-investigating-the-influence-of-vertical-moisture/w:740f03e5-b909-4044-b071-f690715badea/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s40068-024-00375-2

### 1.5 Increased Asian Sulfate Aerosol Emissions Remarkably Enhance Sahel Summer Precipitation

-   **Authors:** Jianing Guo, Xiaoning Xie, and co-authors
-   **Year:** 2024
-   **Description:** Investigates how sulfate aerosol emissions from
    Asia can affect summer rainfall in the Sahel through changes in
    atmospheric circulation and climate processes. It highlights that
    rainfall changes in West Africa can be influenced by factors far
    beyond the region.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/guo-xie-increased-asian-sulfate-aerosol-emissions/w:3bf18af5-3242-411f-a25c-185e97a6b5a0/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2024EF004745

### 1.6 Rainfall Variability, Vegetation Dynamics, and the Rainfall-NDVI Relationship in the Sahel (1990--2022): A Spatio-Temporal Analysis Using CHIRPS and PKU GIMMS-MODIS Data

-   **Author:** Demba Gaye
-   **Year:** 2026
-   **Description:** Examines how rainfall variability relates to
    vegetation dynamics across the Sahel over several decades. It uses
    rainfall and vegetation-index datasets to explore where and when
    vegetation changes track precipitation changes.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/gaye-rainfall-variability-vegetation-dynamics-and-the/w:e9206d4d-8671-4bf2-b5f9-aae1f411c3dc/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1088/2515-7620/ae7ceb

## 2. Soil moisture, wind shear, and convective storms

### 2.1 Soil Moisture Gradients Strengthen Mesoscale Convective Systems by Increasing Wind Shear

-   **Authors:** Emma J. Barton, Cornelia Klein, Christopher M. Taylor,
    and co-authors
-   **Year:** 2025
-   **Description:** Explains how differences in soil moisture across a
    region can strengthen wind shear and help organize large storm
    systems. The findings connect land-surface conditions to storm
    intensity and rainfall hazards.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/barton-klein-soil-moisture-gradients-strengthen-mesoscale/w:64ad190e-b06b-445f-a0b0-33d45ffc9ce1/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-025-01666-8

### 2.2 Wind Shear Enhances Soil Moisture Influence on Rapid Thunderstorm Growth

-   **Authors:** Christopher M. Taylor, Cornelia Klein, Emma J. Barton,
    and co-authors
-   **Year:** 2026
-   **Description:** Investigates how wind shear changes the influence
    of soil moisture on the rapid development of thunderstorms. It helps
    explain why the same soil-moisture pattern may have different
    effects depending on the atmospheric wind structure.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/taylor-klein-wind-shear-enhances-soil-moisture-influence-on/w:09e16f5e-edb3-4397-b187-2a21c995af8a/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10045-7

### 2.3 How Sensitive Are Sahelian Mesoscale Convective Systems to Cold-Pool Suppression?

-   **Authors:** Ben Maybee, and co-authors
-   **Year:** 2025
-   **Description:** Uses a high-resolution storm simulation experiment
    to investigate the role of cold pools---cool air produced by
    evaporating rain---in the life cycle and behavior of Sahelian storm
    systems.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/maybee-bassford-how-sensitive-are-sahelian-mesoscale-convective/w:224ed406-7174-47b6-8c1d-0b372cc37075/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.5032

### 2.4 Environment of Severe Storm Formations over West Africa on 26--28 June 2018

-   **Authors:** Osei, Aryee, and co-authors
-   **Year:** 2023
-   **Description:** Analyses the atmospheric conditions associated with
    two severe storm events over West Africa in June 2018. It considers
    moisture transport, atmospheric circulation, and other conditions
    that supported storm formation.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/osei-aryee-environment-of-severe-storm-formations-over-west/w:e026159d-e1a4-4ef8-8b3a-8f92552ca58d/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1002/met.2109

## 3. Statistical methods for detecting rainfall and climate trends

### 3.1 Modified Mann-Kendall with Higher-Order Statistics for Trend Analysis

-   **Authors:** Yick Jing Then, Syafrina Abdul Halim
-   **Year:** 2025 (published online); journal issue 2026
-   **Description:** Proposes a modified Mann-Kendall approach that
    considers higher-order statistical behavior when testing for trends.
    It addresses limitations that can arise when time-series
    observations are autocorrelated or show nonlinear behavior.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/then-halim-modified-mannkendall-with-higherorder-statistics/w:6e32ea27-0ed2-479c-b2a2-97de73ca17b1/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-30034-0

### 3.2 An Improved Version of the Prewhitening Method for Trend Analysis in the Autocorrelated Time Series

-   **Authors:** Rahul Sheoran, Umesh Chandra Dumka, Rakesh K. Tiwari,
    Rakesh K. Hooda
-   **Year:** 2024
-   **Description:** Presents an improved prewhitening method for time
    series with autocorrelation. This matters because autocorrelation
    can distort the results of trend tests such as Mann-Kendall and lead
    to misleading conclusions about whether a climate variable is
    increasing or decreasing.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/sheoran-dumka-an-improved-version-of-the-prewhitening-method-for/w:b2cf791a-5af7-4284-b5aa-e9886f19bfc8/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos15101159

### 3.3 On the Gaussian Distribution of the Mann-Kendall Tau in the Case of Autocorrelated Data

-   **Authors:** Tristan Gamot, Nils Thibeau-Sutre, Tom J. M. Van Dooren
-   **Year:** 2026
-   **Description:** Examines the statistical distribution of the
    Mann-Kendall trend statistic when observations are autocorrelated.
    This is relevant when assessing whether a detected rainfall trend is
    statistically significant and whether standard assumptions are
    appropriate.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/gamot-thibeausutre-on-the-gaussian-distribution-of-the-mannkendall/w:04593275-8431-4c65-8d43-6dbc10e0aae0/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0333224

### 3.4 On the Robustness of Mann-Kendall Tests Used to Detect Trends in Environmental Time Series

-   **Authors:** Tristan Gamot, Nils Thibeau-Sutre, and co-authors
-   **Year:** Year to verify
-   **Description:** Concerns the reliability of Mann-Kendall trend
    tests when applied to environmental time series. It is relevant to
    choosing and validating a trend-detection method for rainfall,
    temperature, or other long-term Earth-observation variables.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/gamot-thibeausutre-on-the-robustness-of-mannkendall-tests-used-to/w:c85679b9-068f-4e40-ab30-ae6fd9e6b09a/

### 3.5 Seasonally Adjusted Periodic Time Series for Trend Analysis

-   **Authors:** Güçlü, Acar
-   **Year:** Year to verify
-   **Description:** Addresses the analysis of periodic time series
    while accounting for seasonal patterns. This is useful for climate
    variables such as rainfall, which naturally vary by season and can
    produce misleading trend estimates if seasonality is not handled
    correctly.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/g%C3%BC%C3%A7l%C3%BC-acar-seasonally-adjusted-periodic-time-series-for/w:8848f95f-8774-4b53-a745-fe1266696583/

## 4. Satellite precipitation products and observation quality

### 4.1 Cross-Scale Performance Evaluation of GPM IMERG V07 Precipitation Products in a Typical Mountainous Monsoon Region

-   **Authors:** Shaoe Yang, Yanli Chen, Guoxue Xie, Qiting Huang
-   **Year:** 2026
-   **Description:** Evaluates NASA's GPM IMERG V07 satellite rainfall
    estimates against ground rain gauges at daily and monthly scales. It
    shows why satellite precipitation products should be validated
    carefully and why their errors can change when data are aggregated
    over longer periods.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/yang-chen-crossscale-performance-evaluation-of-gpm-imerg-v07/w:fe15563e-2142-4f15-aa37-d0644621e6fa/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18172867

### 4.2 Crowdsourced Data Reveal Shortcomings in Precipitation Phase Products for Rain and Snow Partitioning

-   **Authors:** G. Yu, K. S. Jennings, B. J. Hatchett, A. W. Nolin, H.
    Hur, M. Collins, and co-authors
-   **Year:** 2024
-   **Description:** Uses crowdsourced observations to identify
    limitations in products that distinguish rainfall from snowfall.
    Although its focus is precipitation phase rather than Sahel rainfall
    trends, it is relevant to the broader issue of validating
    precipitation datasets with independent observations.
-   **Consensus:**
    https://consensus.app/papers/yu-jennings-crowdsourced-data-reveal-shortcomings-in/w:924fe623-6e4a-43ca-a153-d5ef8e5c6c0a/
-   **Additional source:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL112853

## Suggested reading priority for a Sahel rainfall investigation

1.  **Regional rainfall trends:** Spät et al. (2025), Rauch et
    al. (2025), and Wane et al. (2025).
2.  **Physical drivers of rainfall and storms:** Adigun et al. (2024),
    Barton et al. (2025), Taylor et al. (2026), and Osei et al. (2023).
3.  **Rainfall and vegetation relationships:** Gaye (2026).
4.  **Satellite rainfall data reliability:** Yang et al. (2026).
5.  **Reliable trend detection:** Sheoran and Dumka (2024), Then and
    Halim (2025/2026), and the two Gamot and Thibeau-Sutre papers.

For an Earth-observation project, a useful methodological combination is
to compare rainfall estimates from a satellite product such as GPM IMERG
or CHIRPS with vegetation indicators such as NDVI, then test long-term
trends while accounting for seasonality and autocorrelation.
